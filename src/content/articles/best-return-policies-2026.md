A great return policy is a quiet money-saver. It turns the wrong size, the gadget you never use or the sofa that looked bigger online into a quick errand instead of a loss. We ranked the most generous policies among the 108 stores we cover, using only rules confirmed on each store's own website.

Length is the headline, but the catch decides how good a policy really is. So every store below comes with the rule that trips people up.

## No time limit: Costco, Patagonia and Sam's Club

Three stores don't put a fixed deadline on most purchases. **Costco** backs most items with its satisfaction guarantee and even refunds the membership fee at any time; electronics and major appliances are the exception at 90 days. **Sam's Club** works the same way for active members, with shorter windows for electronics (90 days) and cell phones (14 days).

**Patagonia** goes furthest for clothing: it accepts returns of anything it sold, including used Worn Wear and sale items, with no set time limit. The fine print is gentle but real: fit and colour returns should be made promptly with tags attached, and items over a year old are reviewed under its Ironclad Guarantee, which doesn't cover normal wear and tear.

## A full year: Chewy, L.L.Bean, IKEA and REI

**Chewy** gives 365 days from the sale date and pays for the return shipping, which is rare for heavy items like pet food. **L.L.Bean** ended its famous unlimited guarantee in 2018 but still gives a year with proof of purchase. **IKEA US** gives a year for unopened products and 180 days for opened or assembled ones, which matters for furniture you only discover is wrong after building it. **REI** gives co-op members a year and everyone else 90 days, so joining before a big gear purchase is worth it.

## A year for members abroad

Outside the US, loyalty programmes unlock the longest windows. **Decathlon France** gives up to 365 days when the purchase is on your free account, against 30 days without one. In Australia, **Kmart** and **Target Australia** give OnePass members 365 days for change-of-mind returns, though anything after day 60 is paid as a voucher or returns card rather than cash.

## The reliable 90-day club

**Target**, **Walmart** and **Kohl's** give 90 days on most items, and Home Depot gives a year if you pay with its card. These are the stores most people shop every week, so their exceptions are worth knowing by heart: electronics drop to 30 days at all three, and wireless phones to 14 days at Walmart.

## How to get the most out of a good policy

1. **Keep proof of purchase.** Even no-limit stores ask for it or look it up. Paying by card or with a loyalty account is the easiest way to never lose it.
2. **Check the category.** Electronics have the shortest windows almost everywhere.
3. **Know what you'll get back.** Late returns often become store credit or a voucher instead of a refund.
4. **Use the checker.** Every store page has a "Can I still return it?" checker that shows your days left and can add the last day to your calendar.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which store has the best return policy in 2026?

Costco, Patagonia and Sam's Club have no fixed time limit on most items. Among stores with a deadline, Chewy, L.L.Bean and IKEA US (unopened) give a full year.

### Does L.L.Bean still have a lifetime return policy?

No. Since 2018, L.L.Bean accepts returns within one year with proof of purchase, and after that only items with a defect in materials or craftsmanship.

### Which store gives the longest return window for electronics?

Costco and Sam's Club give 90 days on electronics, which is longer than Target and Walmart (30 days) and much longer than Apple (14 days) or Best Buy (15 days for most products).

### Are these policies the same in every country?

No. For example, IKEA UAE only accepts unopened items within 90 days, while IKEA US and UK give a year. See [brands by country](/brands/).
