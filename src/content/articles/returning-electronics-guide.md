Electronics are where return policies get strict. Windows shrink to two weeks, opened boxes can cost you a percentage of the price, and phones often have their own rules. If you're buying something expensive, five minutes with this guide can save a lot of money.

## Why electronics windows are short

An opened phone or laptop can't be sold as new, so stores protect themselves with short windows and restocking fees. That's also why some categories are excluded entirely: Jarir and eXtra don't take back phones, computers or video games for a change of mind, only for defects, and Croma in India offers replacement only for defective or damaged items.

## Restocking fees, explained

A restocking fee is a percentage or a flat amount kept from your refund when you return a non-defective item. It almost always applies to **opened** products, which is why the single best habit is to decide before you unwrap. At Samsung US the fee depends on the product type, and Galaxy S and A series phones are exempt. At JB Hi-Fi, sealed items avoid the 20% fee altogether.

Faulty products are different. A defect is covered by the store's warranty process and consumer law in every country we cover, separately from these change-of-mind fees.

## Phones have their own clock

Phones on a contract or plan often get the shortest window of all: 14 days at Walmart and Best Buy US (even for paid members), 14 at Sam's Club and 15 at Best Buy Canada for carrier-plan phones. Before you return a phone anywhere, back it up, sign out of every account and factory reset it. Samsung explicitly requires a reset, and returns can be delayed if Find My or similar locks are still on.

## A checklist before you buy

1. Check the window **for that category**, not the store's headline number.
2. Ask about restocking fees on open-box returns.
3. Keep the box, manuals, cables and accessories until the window closes.
4. Record an unboxing video for expensive items, which helps if something arrives damaged (Croma, for example, wants delivery damage reported within 48 hours).
5. Put the deadline in your calendar from the store's page.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which store has the best return policy for electronics?

Costco and Sam's Club, with 90 days on electronics, and Best Buy Canada, Walmart and Target with 30 days. Apple, Samsung and Best Buy US give 14–15 days unless you're a paid Best Buy member.

### Can I avoid a restocking fee?

Usually yes, by returning the product unopened, or if it's defective. Fees apply to opened, non-defective returns.

### Can I return an opened phone?

Within the window at most stores, yes, but a restocking fee may apply (for example $45 on activatable devices at Best Buy US). In Saudi Arabia, Jarir and eXtra accept phones only if they're defective.
